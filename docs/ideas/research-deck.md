# Research Deck — consulting-grade slides from the research firm

## Problem Statement
How might we turn the firm's research into a presentation-ready deck — insight first, an answer-first storyline, honest visuals and a concrete action plan — that clients can present to their team, investors or their own clients, and that impresses on both content and design?

## Recommended Direction
The deck drives the research instead of being an afterthought. After intake, a **ghost deck** states the expected storyline as hypothesis titles; each carries the exhibit that would prove it, the data it needs, and what would disprove it. Analysts research to confirm **or kill** each slide, and must search for disconfirming evidence. After the critic, a **storyliner** rewrites every title to what the evidence supports, and leads with the most surprising, decision-relevant insight — killed hypotheses become findings, not deleted slides.

An **action-plan module** turns the recommendation into something the client can run: phases, owners, budget, KPIs with targets, decision gates, and risks. Everything lands in one format-free **deck spec**. An **honest exhibit library** decides when a chart is allowed and when a qualitative exhibit is used instead; estimates are visibly marked and every slide carries an evidence-strength badge. A **deck review** checks horizontal logic (titles alone tell the story), vertical logic (each exhibit proves its title), insight quality, and that every number traces to the critic's "safe to use" list.

Renderers turn the spec into a **web deck, a PDF and an editable PowerPoint with native charts**, with **audience cuts** (team, investor/bank, client) and **brand themes**. Decided 2026-10-24: deliver all formats in v1.

## Insight-first rules (the "wow" standard)
- Slide 2 answers the question; nothing important waits until the end.
- Every title is a full-sentence conclusion with a "so what", never a topic label.
- Lead with what's surprising and decision-relevant ("Your real competitors aren't the brands you named").
- One message per slide; one exhibit that proves it; the key data point is highlighted, the rest grey.
- Hero numbers get their own slide when they carry the story.
- Every slide shows its evidence strength and sources; estimates are hatched or labelled.

## Key Assumptions to Validate
- [ ] **Hypotheses don't bias the research** — re-run the coffee case: the "real competitors are in District 3" finding must still appear, and every ledger must show disconfirming searches.
- [ ] **Decks feel consulting-grade** — blind score by 2–3 people with consulting experience on storyline, action titles, exhibit fit, sourcing, actionability, visual quality: target ≥4/5.
- [ ] **Thin data still makes a strong deck** — the coffee deck (no current prices) scores ≥3.5/5 using qualitative exhibits.
- [ ] **PowerPoint output is usable** — opens cleanly in PowerPoint and Keynote, charts are native and editable.
- [ ] **Cost** — log the extra strong-model runs; keep added cost ≤ ~2× the memo cost.

## Scope (v0.4.0)
**In:** ghost deck, prove/disprove research, storyliner, action-plan module, deck spec, exhibit library with data rules, deck review, web/PDF renderer, PowerPoint renderer with native charts, audience cuts, brand themes, a test deck from a real engagement.
**Out:** live data connections, interactive charts beyond the web deck, video/animation, multi-language decks in one file.

## Not Doing (and Why)
- **Charts from estimates by default** — the client's rule is to show thin data honestly; scenario charts only with stated assumptions and visible marking.
- **Free-form slide layouts** — a fixed exhibit library keeps quality checkable and both renderers consistent.
- **Stock imagery and decoration** — consulting decks persuade with evidence and clarity, not pictures.
- **Replacing the memo** — the memo ships alongside as the handout.
