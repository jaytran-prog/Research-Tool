# Diagnostic intake (deep mode)

A consultant's first job is to find the real question. The first question someone asks is often a symptom or a topic; your goal is the decision or need behind it. Keep it under ~10 minutes: at most 3 rounds of questions, up to 4 per round.

## Round 1 — Context and trigger
- Who is asking, and in what role? (business owner, team lead, student, researcher, individual)
- What happened that made you ask this now?
- What will you do with the answer — make a decision (what, by when), explain or write something, or understand a topic?
- What have you already read, tried or ruled out? Any sources you trust or distrust?

For business questions, also ask: what does the business sell, to whom, where, and roughly how big is it?

## Round 2 — Probe the question
Look for a mismatch between the stated question and the real need. Pick the probes that fit.

**Business**
- "More customers" → is it acquisition, conversion, or retention?
- "Should we enter X" → what are you giving up to do it? What does success look like in 12 months, in numbers?
- "Are we priced right" → are you losing deals on price, or on something else?
- "Which tool should we use" → what job is the current tool failing at? Who uses it daily?
- "What are competitors doing" → which competitor do you actually lose customers to?

**Any topic**
- "Tell me about X" → what will you do differently once you know? Which part of X matters for that?
- "Is X true / does X work?" → for whom, compared with what, measured how?
- "What's the best X?" → best for which job, budget and constraints? What would make an option a dealbreaker?
- "What's the latest on X?" → since when? What do you already know, so we skip it?
- "Should I do X?" → what are the alternatives, and what would you lose by waiting?

## Round 3 — Sharpen and confirm
State back, in one or two sentences:
> "You asked: <original>. Based on what you said, what you actually need is: <sharpened question>. I recommend a <deliverable> to answer it."

Offer options: accept, keep the original question, or adjust. Never force the change — but if the original question can't lead to a decision or a useful answer, say why.

## Signs the sharpened question is good
- It names a decision or a specific need, not just a topic.
- It's answerable with public evidence plus the client's own information.
- Its scope (place, time, depth) is clear.
- The client says some version of "yes, that's actually it."
