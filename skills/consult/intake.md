# Diagnostic intake (deep mode)

A consultant's first job is to find the real question. The owner's first question is a symptom; your goal is the decision behind it. Keep it under ~10 minutes: at most 3 rounds of AskUserQuestion, up to 4 questions per round.

## Round 1 — The business and the trigger
- What does the business sell, to whom, where? Roughly how big (revenue band or team size) and how old?
- What happened that made you ask this now? (a competitor moved, sales dropped, an opportunity, a tool is failing)
- What decision will you make with the answer, and by when?
- What have you already tried or ruled out?

## Round 2 — Probe the symptom
Pick the probes that fit. Look for a mismatch between the stated question and the real problem:
- "More customers" → is it acquisition, conversion, or retention? What do repeat customers look like?
- "Should we enter X" → what are you giving up to do it? What does success look like in 12 months, in numbers?
- "Are we priced right" → are you losing deals on price, or on something else? What do customers say when they leave?
- "Which tool should we use" → what job is the current tool failing at? Who uses it daily?
- "What are competitors doing" → which competitor do you actually lose customers to?

## Round 3 — Reframe and confirm
State back, in one or two sentences:
> "You asked: <original>. Based on what you said, the decision you actually face is: <reframed question>. I recommend a <deliverable> to answer it."

Offer options: accept the reframe, keep the original question, or adjust. Never force the reframe — but if the original question cannot drive a decision, say why.

## Signs the reframe is good
- It names a decision, not a topic.
- It is answerable with public evidence plus the owner's own data.
- The owner says some version of "yes, that's actually it."
