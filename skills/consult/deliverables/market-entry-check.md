# Market Entry Check

**Answers:** Should I enter this new market, location, customer segment, or product line — go, no-go, or test first?
**Best for:** expansion decisions with real money or time at stake.

## Quick-form questions
1. Your business today: what you sell, where, roughly how big?
2. Which market are you considering? (a new city, segment, product, or channel)
3. How much money and time could you put in, and what would success look like in 12 months?
4. What makes you think this market is attractive?
5. Memo language?

## Workstreams
| id | Questions | Suggested sources | Budget |
|----|-----------|-------------------|--------|
| `demand` | Is there real demand? Size signals, growth, search interest, customer counts. Bottom-up estimate where possible. | Official statistics, industry associations, Google Trends, marketplace volumes, local news | 7 searches · 7 fetches |
| `competition` | Who already serves this market, how crowded is it, and how strong are they? | Google Maps, directories, marketplaces, review platforms | 6 searches · 6 fetches |
| `barriers` | What does it take to enter? Licences, regulation, capital, suppliers, staff, typical setup costs and timelines. | Regulators, government portals, supplier sites, job boards, industry guides | 6 searches · 6 fetches |
| `economics` | Typical prices, margins and customer acquisition costs; how long until breakeven for a business this size. | Pricing pages, industry benchmarks, franchise disclosures, public filings of similar businesses | 6 searches · 6 fetches |

## Critic checklist
- Market size figures state geography, year, and method; top-down numbers are not presented as the owner's addressable market.
- The bottom-up estimate's arithmetic is reproducible.
- Regulatory claims come from an official source or are flagged as unverified.
- The economics are adjusted to the owner's scale, not to industry leaders.
